Teaching

Current Courses (Dankook University)

International Relations

Provides a broad picture of how today's world works and how we interact with the world. Begins with three representative IR theories, then covers diverse topics such as international organizations, human rights, the natural environment, information and communication technologies, and war and violent conflict.

Emerging Issues in International Relations

An "emerging issue" is one that migrates from low politics into the domain of high politics — and cyberspace is the exemplary case. The course traces that migration: the history of the internet and the development of Korean internet governance, the contested rise of the multistakeholder model, digital trade and data governance, competitive cybersecurity multilateralism, and the evolution of national cyber strategy and military operational concepts. It closes on the nexus of data, supply chain, and platform security, public–private cooperation, AI and global governance, and digital authoritarianism. Students finish by applying the semester's concepts and theories to explain events unfolding in the present.

International Political Economy

Learning IPE is now essential to understanding not only economics but also the politics of the world, since it has become increasingly difficult to fully understand one side without knowing the other. Designed to help students understand the nexus of economics, politics, and international relations from the perspective of IPE.

Foreign Policy Analysis

Theory here is a tool for explaining foreign policy as it is actually made, not an end in itself. The course moves from the objectives of foreign policy and the question of national interest to the machinery that produces it — domestic politics, bureaucratic and organizational models, and the decision-making system itself. It then works through the substantive arenas of South Korean foreign policy: relations with the United States, policy toward North Korea, military and security policy, relations with Japan, multilateral diplomacy through international organizations, and development cooperation. The emphasis throughout falls on Korea, so that the analytical tools are tested against the environment students actually live in.
